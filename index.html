PMT Score Check — Apna Ghar Apni Chhat: In a Joint Family, Whose CNIC Decides the Score?

Last updated: 19 November 2026.

Apna Ghar Apni Chhat asks for a PMT Score Check before it decides whether your household qualifies for a home. In a single small family that question is simple. In a joint family — parents, married sons, grandchildren under one roof — it is not, because the score follows one person: the head of the household as the survey record names them. Check the score with the wrong CNIC and you will get the wrong answer, even if the household itself qualifies. The PMT Score Check guide on pmtscorecheck.com explains the check itself; this page is about whose CNIC counts in a joint family.

Whose CNIC counts

The score is calculated on the household record, and the record has one head. That is usually the person registered as the head in the PSER or BISP survey — most often the eldest earning member, or the person in whose name the household was surveyed. If you are not sure who that is, the tehsil office can tell you from the record. Do not guess: checking your own CNIC when the record names your father or your elder brother as head will show you a different household's answer, or no answer at all. Send the head's 13-digit CNIC by SMS to 8171, or check it through the 8171 web portal, and use a SIM registered in that person's name. Keep the reply message and the date it arrived.

What the score decides for the housing scheme

The scheme does not look at one person's income. The score reads the whole household record — how many people live in the house, what they earn together, whether the family owns a house already, and what the survey recorded about the home. That is why two brothers living in the same house can get different answers if they check with different CNICs: the record behind each CNIC is not the same household. The score that matters is the one tied to the head of the household, because that is the record the scheme reads.

If the head of the household has changed

Joint families change. A head passes away, a son moves out and sets up his own household, or the person named in the record no longer lives there. When that happens, the record is out of date and the score follows the old record until it is corrected. Take the CNICs of the adults in the house, the children's B-Forms and a recent utility bill to your nearest BISP tehsil office and ask for a re-survey, so the record names the right head. The correction is free, and the survey is a government exercise. After the correction, wait a few days and check again through 8171 before you apply — the new record needs time to reach the system.

Before you apply

Check the score first, then apply with the same CNIC you checked with. If the reply says the household is eligible, apply with the head's CNIC and keep the reply message with your papers. If the reply says the household is not eligible, do not pay anyone to fix it — the record is what needs checking, not the application. The detailed Apna Ghar Apni Chhat PMT Score Verification guide sets out how the score and the housing scheme are connected, step by step.

Before you act on any answer

Whatever the reply says, check it once more with the same CNIC before you leave the house — a single check can carry a typing mistake. Do not forward the reply to agents or groups, and do not share your CNIC with anyone who offers to check it for you. The check is free, the re-survey is free, and the official helpline is 0800-26477.

A note on staying safe

Never pay anyone who promises to raise your PMT Score Check result or arrange an Apna Ghar Apni Chhat approval for a fee, and never share your CNIC with unofficial apps or strangers who claim they can add your name to a list. Your score comes only from the official survey record.
